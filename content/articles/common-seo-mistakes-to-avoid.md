# Common SEO Mistakes to Avoid: What Local Businesses Get Wrong

Most local businesses don't lose at SEO because they're up against impossible odds. They lose because they make the same preventable mistakes — errors that push them below competitors who aren't necessarily better, just more careful.

These are the most common and costly SEO mistakes local businesses make. Each one is fixable, and fixing them can make a real difference in where you show up on Google.

## Mistake #1: Writing Title Tags That Don't Help You

Your title tag is the blue headline people click on in Google search results. It's one of your most powerful tools for attracting clicks and telling Google what your page is about.

**Too long or too short:** Google shows about 60 characters. Anything more gets chopped off with "..." which looks unprofessional and reduces clicks. Too short wastes space.

**Brand name first:** Writing "ABC Roofing | Roof Repair San Antonio" is backwards. Google gives more weight to the first words, and so do people scanning results. Unless people search for your brand name, lead with what they're actually looking for.

**Repeating keywords:** "Roof Repair San Antonio | Roof Repair Experts" looks spammy to both Google and customers. It's an outdated tactic that now works against you.

**Fix:** Keep titles at 50-60 characters. Lead with your main keyword, put your brand name at the end. Use your keyword once, naturally.

## Mistake #2: Ignoring Meta Descriptions

Meta descriptions don't directly affect rankings, but they heavily influence whether people click your result.

**The problem:** Many businesses leave meta descriptions blank or duplicate the same one across every page. Google may then show "no description available" or pull random text from your page. Neither makes people want to click.

**Fix:** Write a unique 150-160 character description for every page. Include your keyword — Google bolds it in results, which draws the eye. Add a call to action like "Get a free estimate" or "Learn how." Well-written descriptions are used by Google about 70% of the time.

## Mistake #3: Messing Up Page Headers

Headers (H1, H2, H3 tags) tell Google the structure and topic of your content. Getting them wrong confuses both Google and visitors.

**Multiple H1 tags:** Every page should have exactly one H1 — the main headline. Some sites use H1s for logos, navigation, or multiple sections. This makes it harder for Google to understand the page.

**Same keyword in every header:** If your H1 is "Roof Repair San Antonio" and your H2s repeat variations, Google detects this as keyword stuffing.

**Fix:** One H1 per page containing your primary keyword. Use H2s for major sections with related keyword variations. H3s for subsections. Think of it like a newspaper — one main headline, then clear subheadlines.

## Mistake #4: Treating Images Like an Afterthought

Images are a major SEO opportunity that most local businesses completely ignore. Google can't see photos — it relies on text to understand them.

**Generic file names:** Uploading IMG_4532.jpg or screenshot.png tells Google nothing. File names are a real ranking factor. Rename images descriptively before uploading: `commercial-roof-repair.jpg` instead of `IMG_4532.jpg`.

**Missing or lazy alt text:** Alt text describes images for search engines and screen readers. "Image" or "photo" is useless. Write descriptions that actually convey what the image shows. Include your keyword only when it naturally fits — forced keywords can trigger penalties.

**Massive file sizes:** Uploading images straight from a camera without compressing them can single-handedly kill your page speed. Keep images under 100KB. Use WebP format, which is 25-35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality. Use lazy loading for images below the fold.

## Mistake #5: Creating Messy URLs

Your URLs are another signal Google uses to understand your content.

**Using numbers and parameters:** `yourwebsite.com/page?id=123` tells Google nothing. `yourwebsite.com/roof-repair-san-antonio/` tells Google exactly what the page is about.

**Changing URLs without redirects:** If you update a URL without setting up a redirect, anyone who bookmarked or linked to the old URL hits a dead page. You also lose any authority that page had built up.

**Fix:** Use short, descriptive, lowercase URLs with hyphens between words. If you must change a URL, always set up a 301 redirect from the old to the new one.

## Mistake #6: Neglecting Mobile Experience

Google uses mobile-first indexing — it evaluates the mobile version of your site first for rankings.

**Tiny touch targets:** Buttons and links that are too small or too close together make your site frustrating on a phone. Google requires touch targets of at least 48x48 pixels.

**Intrusive popups:** Full-screen popups blocking content on mobile get demoted by Google.

**Slow mobile load times:** Mobile users are often on cellular connections. If your site is slow, they're gone. Test regularly on an actual phone and compress images to improve load times.

## Mistake #7: Not Using HTTPS

If your website URL starts with "http://" instead of "https://," visitors see a "Not Secure" warning. That warning scares away customers and hurts your Google rankings.

Even with HTTPS set up, make sure every image, script, and font on your page also loads securely. Mixed content — some secure, some not — breaks the padlock icon.

**Fix:** Ensure your entire site uses HTTPS. After switching, set up 301 redirects from all old HTTP URLs to the new secure versions.

## Mistake #8: Overlooking Internal Links

An orphan page — a page nothing else on your site links to — is essentially invisible to Google. Google discovers pages by following links. No links means Google may never find it.

**Fix:** Every important page should be linked from at least one other page. Service pages should be linked from your homepage. Blog posts should link to relevant service pages. Build a logical web of connections throughout your site.

## Mistake #9: Blocking Google from Key Resources

Some websites accidentally block Google from seeing their CSS or JavaScript files through a robots.txt configuration. Google needs these files to properly render and understand your pages. If it can't see the full picture, it can't rank you properly.

**Fix:** Make sure your robots.txt file doesn't block CSS or JavaScript. These resources are essential for Google to understand how your page works.

## The Bottom Line

Most SEO mistakes aren't caused by a lack of sophisticated strategy. They're caused by overlooking basics — title tags, page speed, image optimization, mobile experience, and proper site structure. The businesses ranking at the top of Google aren't doing anything magical. They're simply avoiding these common errors and consistently getting the fundamentals right.

Start with the easiest fixes — title tags, meta descriptions, and image alt text — then work toward bigger improvements. Every correction moves you closer to the top.
