# Schema Markup for Local Businesses: A Plain-English Guide to Structured Data

When you search for a local restaurant, you've probably seen search results that show star ratings, hours of operation, pricing information, and frequently asked questions right on the Google results page — without even clicking through to a website.

That's not magic. It's **schema markup** — also called structured data — and it's one of the most underused tools in local SEO. Adding schema markup to your website helps Google understand your business better and display richer, more prominent listings in search results. For local service businesses, this can mean the difference between a search result that gets ignored and one that gets clicked.

## What Is Schema Markup?

Schema markup is a standardized code format you add to your website to give search engines specific information about your business. Think of it as a label system that tells Google exactly what everything on your page means.

Without schema markup, Google has to guess which text is your business name, your address, your phone number, or your services. Schema removes the guesswork by explicitly identifying each piece of information.

Google uses this to create **rich results** — enhanced search listings with star ratings, FAQ answers, business hours, pricing, and more. Rich results stand out on the search page and get significantly more clicks.

## How Schema Markup Works

Schema markup uses a format called JSON-LD. You don't need to understand the code yourself — your web developer can implement it — but it helps to understand the concept.

JSON-LD is essentially a snippet of code placed in your website that contains structured information about your business. It sits behind the scenes and doesn't change how your website looks to visitors. It only communicates with search engines.

For example, a local business schema might tell Google:

- The name of your business
- Your exact address
- Your phone number
- Your business hours
- The geographic coordinates of your location
- Your price range
- What type of business you are

## Schema Types Every Local Business Should Use

### LocalBusiness Schema

This is the foundation for any local service business. It tells Google detailed information about your business that can appear in search results and the Knowledge Panel — the information box that appears on the right side of Google when someone searches for your business by name.

LocalBusiness schema includes key details like your business name, address, phone number, hours of operation, and geographic coordinates. Those coordinates help Google verify your exact location, which strengthens your local search presence.

Always use the most specific business type available. Instead of the generic "LocalBusiness," use "Dentist," "Restaurant," "LegalService," "Plumber," or whatever most precisely describes your business. This specificity helps Google match your listing to the right searches.

Adding a price range to your schema can display in your Knowledge Panel, giving customers helpful information before they visit your website.

### FAQ Schema

FAQ schema marks up the frequently asked questions and answers on your website. When implemented correctly, it can display expandable questions and answers directly in your search results.

This is one of the highest-impact schema types because it dramatically increases the amount of space your search result occupies on the page. A bigger, more informative listing naturally attracts more attention and clicks.

Important rules for FAQ schema:
- The questions and answers must be visible on the page. Hidden FAQ content is considered spam by Google.
- Keep it to roughly ten questions maximum, as Google typically doesn't display more than that.
- Write genuinely helpful questions that your customers actually ask.

### Article and BlogPosting Schema

If your business publishes blog posts or articles (which you should for SEO), Article schema helps Google display your content with richer information in search results — including the headline, author name, publication date, and last modified date.

The publication date is required. Without it, your article won't be eligible for rich results. The modified date appears in search results when it's different from the publication date, which signals to searchers that the content is current and up to date.

## Common Schema Mistakes to Avoid

Schema markup is powerful, but implementing it incorrectly can backfire. Here are the most common pitfalls:

### Marking Up Content That Doesn't Exist on the Page

Your schema markup must match what's actually visible on the page. If your schema says your business has a five-star rating but that rating isn't displayed on the page, Google considers this spam. The information in your schema and the information on your page must be consistent.

### Using Fake Reviews or Ratings

Never create schema markup with fabricated review data. Google detects fake review patterns and penalizes businesses that use them. Self-serving reviews — where you review your own business — are also explicitly classified as spam.

### Using the Wrong Schema Type for Your Content

Don't add Product schema to an informational page that doesn't sell anything. Don't mark the same content as both an Article and a BlogPosting. Choose the one correct schema type for each page's content.

### Forgetting to Update Your Schema

When you update content on your page — especially dates, prices, or services — make sure your schema markup reflects those changes. Outdated schema creates a mismatch between what Google expects and what visitors find.

## How to Test Your Schema Markup

Before and after implementing schema markup, test it to make sure it's working correctly:

- **Google's Rich Results Test**: This tool shows you which rich results your schema is eligible for. Not all schema types trigger rich results, so this tells you what you can expect.
- **Schema Validator** from schema.org: This catches syntax errors in your code that might prevent Google from reading it properly.

Both tools are free and straightforward to use.

## Why Schema Markup Matters for Local Businesses

Most local businesses haven't implemented schema markup, which means those that do gain a significant competitive advantage. Here's what it delivers:

- **More prominent search listings**: Rich results take up more space and include eye-catching elements like star ratings and FAQ dropdowns
- **Higher click-through rates**: A listing with reviews, hours, and pricing gets more clicks than a plain text result
- **Better understanding by Google**: Schema removes ambiguity, helping Google match your business to more relevant searches
- **Knowledge Panel accuracy**: Schema helps ensure your business information appears correctly when people search for you by name

## Getting Started

Start with LocalBusiness schema on your homepage — name, address, phone, hours, business type, and geographic coordinates. Then add FAQ schema to pages that already have questions and answers visible. If you publish blog content, add Article schema with proper dates.

Work with your web developer to implement schema using JSON-LD format. Test with Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Validator to confirm it's error-free. Then monitor your search results over the following weeks to see enhanced listings appear.

Schema markup is a one-time implementation that delivers ongoing value — making it one of the smartest technical investments a local business can make.
